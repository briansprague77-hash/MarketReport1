## Executive Summary

Tampa Bay Real Estate's New Development Market Report speaks with the voice of an experienced broker who respects agents' intelligence and refuses to waste their time. The brand exists in direct opposition to developer-sponsored marketing materials — it provides independently sourced, data-backed intelligence that working realtors can actually use with their buyers.

The core personality is that of a sharp, no-nonsense colleague who happens to be obsessed with data accuracy. Content should feel like getting a briefing from the most informed person in the room — someone who cites their sources, flags uncertainty, and never tries to sell you on a narrative. The reader should walk away thinking "finally, someone who treats me like a professional."

Voice is constant — it doesn't change by channel or audience.

| We Are | We Are Not |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Attribute 1: Direct
- **What it means**: Say what the data shows without hedging, softening, or burying the lead. If a development is overpriced relative to comps, say so. If absorption is slow, report it.
- **How it shows up**: Short declarative sentences. Lead with the number, then context. No throat-clearing introductions.
- **What to avoid**: Weasel words ("somewhat," "arguably"), passive voice that hides accountability, long windup paragraphs before the point.
- **Evidence**: Homepage hero — "The Market Report Your Buyers Deserve" — implies existing reports don't deserve their time.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Attribute 2: Anti-Marketing
- **What it means**: Actively reject the conventions of luxury real estate marketing. No lifestyle imagery descriptions, no "stunning waterfront views," no developer press-release language.
- **How it shows up**: The "Straight Talk" section on homepage. Explicit callouts like "No spin. No 'luxury lifestyle' filler. Just sourced data."
- **What to avoid**: Adjective-heavy descriptions of amenities, aspirational lifestyle framing, any language that sounds like it came from a developer's PR firm.
- **Evidence**: Homepage copy — "No spin. No luxury lifestyle filler. Just sourced data." and tagline "Built by a broker who got tired of sending clients marketing flyers instead of real data."
- **Confidence**: High

#### Attribute 3: Sourced
- **What it means**: Every price, every percentage, every trend claim links back to a named data source. MLS, DBPR filings, public records, developer disclosures — always cited.
- **How it shows up**: Inline citations (e.g., "per Stellar MLS"), data vintage dates, methodology notes, source appendices on reports.
- **What to avoid**: Uncited statistics, vague attributions ("industry sources say"), round numbers presented as precise data.
- **Evidence**: Market data components consistently label sources. Development profiles include filing references.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Attribute 4: Independent
- **What it means**: No developer pays for placement or favorable coverage. Analysis is broker-produced, not builder-funded. This is the core trust differentiator.
- **How it shows up**: Explicit independence statements. Willingness to report unfavorable data about any development. No "featured" or "sponsored" tiers.
- **What to avoid**: Anything that could be read as promotional for a specific developer. Omitting negative data points. Uneven coverage depth that implies favoritism.
- **Evidence**: Brand positioning throughout — "not sponsored," independence as a messaging pillar.
- **Confidence**: High

#### Attribute 5: Agent-First
- **What it means**: The primary audience is working real estate agents who need to advise buyers. Content is built for professionals making business decisions, not consumers browsing for fun.
- **How it shows up**: AudienceContext `isPro` toggle. Technical metrics (AES, DVR, PSF) presented without dumbing down. Comparison tools designed for client presentations.
- **What to avoid**: Consumer-magazine tone. Over-explaining basic real estate concepts to the professional audience. Lifestyle-first framing.
- **Evidence**: Audience toggle in app architecture. Market report sections designed as agent briefing tools.
- **Confidence**: High

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Independence**
   - Core idea: This report isn't paid for by developers — the analysis is unbiased.
   - When to use: Introductions, about sections, any context where credibility matters.
   - Example phrasing: "Not sponsored. Not developer-funded. Built by a broker for brokers."

2. **Data-First**
   - Core idea: Every claim is cited, every number is sourced, every trend is verifiable.
   - When to use: Market analysis sections, methodology notes, any data presentation.
   - Example phrasing: "Every claim cited. Every price sourced."

3. **Agent Utility**
   - Core idea: This exists to make agents better at their job — it's a tool, not content marketing.
   - When to use: Feature descriptions, onboarding, value propositions to new users.
   - Example phrasing: "Built for working agents who need real intel, not marketing flyers."

- vs. Generic market reports: We specialize in Tampa Bay new construction — depth over breadth.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

Voice is constant. Tone flexes by context. These dimensions adjust:

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Market report sections | High | Medium | High | Precision and completeness |
| Development detail pages | Medium-High | Medium | Medium-High | Comprehensive but scannable |
| Homepage / marketing pages | Medium | High | Low-Medium | Hook fast, establish credibility |
| Social media | Low-Medium | High | Low | Brevity and one sharp insight |
| Agent email communications | Medium | Warm | Medium | Respect their time, add value |
| Blog / editorial content | Medium | Medium-High | Medium | Teach, don't lecture |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Market Report Sections
- **Overall tone**: Authoritative analyst briefing — dense but organized.
- **Opening approach**: Lead with the most important number or trend.
- **Do's**: Use precise figures, cite sources inline, include data vintage dates, present both bullish and bearish signals.
- **Don'ts**: Editorialize beyond what data supports, use marketing language, bury the lead in methodology.
- **Example**: "Pinellas new construction pipeline: 1,247 units across 9 active developments. Absorption rate: 4.2 units/month county-wide (Stellar MLS, Q4 2025)."

#### Development Detail Pages
- **Overall tone**: Thorough research dossier on a single project.
- **Opening approach**: Name, location, status, key differentiating metric.
- **Do's**: Include PSF ranges, unit mix, delivery timelines, absorption data, filing references.
- **Don'ts**: Use developer marketing copy, describe lifestyle/amenities in aspirational language, omit unfavorable comps.

#### Social Media
- **Overall tone**: Sharp, punchy data drops that earn a follow.
- **Opening approach**: One surprising number or counterintuitive insight.
- **Do's**: One stat per post, cite the source, invite questions, link to full report.
- **Don'ts**: Thread long analyses, use hashtag spam, post lifestyle photos of buildings, sound like a developer ad.
- **Example**: "Tampa Bay new construction PSF hit $X in Q4. Here's what that means for your next buyer consult →"

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| PSF | Price per square foot — primary pricing metric | "price per foot," "$/ft" | "Listed at $485 PSF per Stellar MLS" |
| AES | Absorption Efficiency Score — sell-through speed | "sales pace," "how fast it's selling" | "AES of 0.72 indicates strong absorption" |
| DVR | Delivery velocity range — months to sell out | "time to sellout" | "DVR: 14-18 months at current pace" |
| DOM | Days on market | "time on market" | "Average DOM: 47 days (MLS, Jan 2026)" |

### Preferred Terms
| Term | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| Pipeline | All new construction from pre-sales through delivered | "The Pinellas pipeline includes 9 active developments" |
| Pre-sales | Units being sold before construction starts | "Pre-sales launched Q3 2025" |
| Absorption | Rate at which units sell over time | "Monthly absorption: 4.2 units" |
| Delivery cycle | Period when completed units transfer to buyers | "Delivery cycle began January 2026" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| "Luxury" (as standalone descriptor) | Overused marketing term, means nothing specific | Use specific: "priced at $X PSF" or "branded residences" |
| "Stunning" / "breathtaking" | Lifestyle copy — not data language | Describe the actual feature or metric |
| "Investment opportunity" | Sounds like a sales pitch, not analysis | "Current pricing relative to comps suggests..." |
| "Hot market" | Vague, unquantified | "Absorption rate of X units/month" |

### Never-Use Terms
| Term | Reason |
|------|--------|
| "Exclusive offer" | We don't sell units — we analyze them |
| "Act now" / urgency language | We're analysts, not salespeople |
| "Guaranteed" (re: returns/appreciation) | Legal liability and contrary to data-driven voice |
